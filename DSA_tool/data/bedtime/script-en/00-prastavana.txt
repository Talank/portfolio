@algo dui-prashna
Whatever the problem is, ask it two questions. The first question is this — have I already done this piece of work once before? If I have, then that answer should be written down somewhere, and I should not count it again. The second question is this — can I throw away half of the remaining work right now? If I can, then even a very large problem finishes in a few steps. These two questions carry you half the way there on their own.

Now let me answer a question, one that a lot of people ask. Why should anyone learn these things? Because they are not for an exam. They are ways of thinking.

However large the problems in your life get, they are usually one of two kinds. Either they can be broken into smaller pieces. Or there is a repeating shape hidden somewhere inside them. These twenty islands teach you to see those same two things.

Listen to one word now, because it comes back many times later. The word is input size, and it means how big the work is. Cooking rice for one person and cooking rice for a hundred people is the same work, but the size is different. Everything in algorithms turns around that size.

Another word is trade. Usually you have to spend space to save time, or spend time to save space. Only sometimes is there a road that saves both, and when you find it, it feels very sweet.

Listen to it like this. When a problem comes, do not try to solve it straight away, just look at its shape. Say to yourself — [aside]"Ah, there are two hands moving here."[/] Or say — [aside]"Ah, half of it is being thrown away here."[/] Recognising that much is enough, and in the morning your fingers do the rest.

Let us also talk slowly about interviews, because that word makes many people tense. The truth is that the questions asked in an interview are these same twenty shapes wearing different clothes. Once you know the shape, the question does not feel new.

Get to know your crewmates too, because they will be with you all night. Luffy always looks for the simple road, and takes whatever is biggest right now. That is greedy thinking.

Zoro works from both ends with two hands. Nami remembers by writing it down, and whatever has been counted once she does not count again. When Usopp loses the road he goes back and looks for another one. Robin goes down into the depth and looks, and then comes back up. Sanji puts things in order.
